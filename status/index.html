    Project status¶

    November 2026. What exists, what has been measured, and what has not. Earlier work is kept in the research log.

    What the project delivers¶

    Part State
    Segmentation of the trapped cell: a U-Net (384 px native crop) and an nnU-Net benchmark trained and tested at the level of whole cells
    Feature extraction with five per-frame quality checks (a frame that fails is not used) working
    HER2 classifier: step-level and cell-level logistic regression, averaged validated by leave-one-cell-out on 15 cells
    Desktop app that analyzes a recorded video and reports the HER2 class with its evidence working; ships no models, see the app
    Real-time operation, connection to the instrument's control software not done: the app analyses recordings (see below)

    Results¶

    Every number below holds out whole cells: frames of one cell are never in both training and test, because frames of the same cell are almost identical. 15 cells (9 high HER2, 6 low HER2), one cell line (SKBR3), one setup.

    Segmentation: Dice score of the trapped cell, mean over cells ± standard deviation

    Model Test protocol Mean over cells High HER2 Low HER2 Worst cell
    U-Net, 384 px crop 5 rounds, random cells held out 0.855 ± 0.114 0.802 0.934 0.625
    U-Net, 384 px crop leave-one-cell-out, 15 rounds 0.865 ± 0.092 0.834 0.910 0.710
    nnU-Net (2D) leave-one-cell-out, 15 rounds 0.940 ± 0.069 0.919 0.972 0.725

    High-HER2 cells are harder to segment than low-HER2 cells. The U-Net takes about 40 ms per frame on a modern GPU, the nnU-Net about 0.4–0.5 s. A cell-level result varies a lot between cells, so a difference of a few hundredths between two single runs is not evidence of anything (see the research log for how large the noise is).

    Classification: HER2 class of a cell

    Using masks from the nnU-Net, the classifier called 14 of 15 cells correctly in leave-one-cell-out testing (AUC 0.94, permutation p ≈ 0.002). It was then fitted on all 15 cells; that final model is what the app uses.

    Limits you should know about¶

    • One setup, 15 cells. The result says how well the method works on cells recorded the way these were (a laser ramp from 500 to 1200 mA in 50 mA steps, 30 s per level). It does not show that it transfers to other instruments, cell lines or protocols.
    • A recording from a different setup may not work. On four recordings from a newer setup (colour camera, a 298–1000 mA ramp in steps of about 9 s) the U-Net found the cell in only 2–13 % of the measured frames, so the app correctly gave no verdict. Using it there needs new annotated frames and fine-tuning.
    • The app is cautious on purpose. It gives a verdict only when at least 3 laser steps are usable and 80 % of the measured frames pass the checks. The classifier was validated on whole recordings (10 or more steps), so a recording of 3 to 9 steps gets a result labelled short recording from the step model alone, which is weaker. An optional provisional verdict exists for recordings that fail the checks; it is always labelled not validated and is not a result.
    • Not real-time. The U-Net is faster than the camera's 45.5 ms frame interval, but the delivered software analyses recordings and is not connected to the instrument.
    • Research software. It is not a medical device and not for clinical decisions.

    What would come next¶

    More cells and a second setup for external validation; fine-tuning for the newer recording protocol; and, if wanted, a version that runs alongside the instrument.
