    Research log: segmentation, September 2026¶

    Historical record, not the current state

    This page is the project's status as of 17–23 September 2026, kept as a record of how the segmentation work developed (run-to-run variance, the per-cell collapse, why the evaluation changed). Later work replaced its protocol (random whole-cell cross-validation, a 384 px native crop, nnU-Net, the classifier and the desktop app). For where the project stands now, read Project status.

    Current model¶

    checkpoints/postswap/experiment_0a_seed_baseline/postswap_ftl_cw142_s42/unet_best.pt · config configs/postswap/experiment_0a_seed_baseline/postswap_ftl_cw142_s42.yaml

    Focal–Tversky loss (α 0.7, β 0.3, γ 0.75, Abraham & Khan 2019) · class weights [1, 4, 2] · width 64 · cosine · scale augmentation only · 451-frame pool.

    Development set (4 cells, 203 annotated frames, fully annotated 23 September):

    evaluated DSC spread
    single model, 4-seed mean 0.809 sd 0.049
    ensemble of four 0.859 —

    Two of four runs collapse on a single cell — a different cell each time

    run worst cell its DSC internal val convergence check
    s42 low-6 0.718 0.9327 passed
    s43 low-6 0.804 0.9398 passed
    s44 high-1 0.507 0.9050 FLAGGED
    s45 high-9 0.630 0.9357 passed ✗

    Neither cell size (high-1 678 px, high-9 704 px) nor internal validation predicts which cell goes. check_convergence.py caught s44 — behind on three of four cells — and missed s45, which trained normally and then lost one cell. Passing it is not evidence.

    Ensembling is what fixes this. No per-cell collapse survives averaging: high-1 never falls below 0.823, high-9 never below 0.822.

    high-1 high-9 low-5 low-6 All cells
    single model, 4-seed mean 0.742 0.792 0.890 0.770 0.809
    ensemble of 4 0.823 0.848 0.920 0.818 0.859

    But an ensemble is an inference-time construct, not a model — no checkpoint, 4× GPU, and the per-frame helper takes one UNet. Averaging the weights instead gives DSC 0.000: independently seeded networks sit in different loss basins and their hidden units are not aligned. Distillation — training one model on the ensemble's soft outputs — is the route to a single deployable checkpoint, and is not yet done.

    What changed on 17 September¶

    The evaluation roster was restructured. high-10 moved into training and high-1 into the development set, because the development cells were measurably smaller than the training cells and the model was being judged on cells smaller than anything it had trained on.

    Measured now, training spans 688–1449 px and the development set 678–1033 px, and the two interleave rather than separating: high-10 (688) sits between high-1 (678) and high-9 (704) — the intended effect.

    Correction, 23 September

    high-1 was re-annotated when its remaining frames were completed, and now measures 678 px, not the 1549 px recorded while it was a training cell. It is the smallest cell in the study, not the largest. The earlier masks no longer exist, so the annotations cannot be compared directly.

    This weakens the premise above: the pre-exchange claim that every development cell was smaller than every training cell assumed a training minimum of 824 px, which depended on high-1 being large. The size gap was real but smaller than reported, and part of it was an annotation artifact. The effect on the two cells that did not move is measured directly and does not depend on it.

    The effect on the two cells that did not move, and so remain comparable:

    Cell before after
    high-9 0.600 0.845 ± 0.043 +0.245 — well outside the noise
    low-6 0.749 0.766 ± 0.044 inside the noise

    One caveat on that result

    high_cell10 (688 px) and high_cell9 (704 px) are near-twins — same laser regime, nearly the same size. Moving high-10 into training was close to adding a duplicate of high-9, so 0.845 is likely optimistic as an estimate for a genuinely new high-power cell. The mechanism is real; the effect size will not transfer intact.

    Run-to-run variance was measured for the first time, and it is large — see the Workflow Guide rules and the banner on Training Results.

    The loss function was ruled out as the bottleneck. Three interventions — class-weight rebalancing, excluding background from the Dice term, and replacing Dice with Focal–Tversky — produced 0.676, 0.675 and 0.691 against a 0.668 baseline. A structural finding along the way: the objective is 0.3·CE + 0.7·Dice, and class_weights reaches only the CE term, so that knob was never touching 70% of the loss.

    Where the evidence points¶

    Object scale, not hyperparameters. At 256×256 the trapped cell occupies 41–90 px — roughly a 6×6 to 9×9 blob — and the decoy class on high-10 about 51 px. That is very likely why no loss function recovered the decoy class: the information may not survive the 17× downsample. Resolution has not been revisited since the 128→256 ablation (+0.048, on 40 masks); the pool is now 451.

    Next¶

    1. Decide what the deployed model is — ensemble (0.859, no checkpoint, 4× GPU) or a distilled single model. This determines which number the thesis reports as "the model".
    2. Test resolution (384, then 512 if the trend holds), with a matched-batch-size control. The development set is now complete, so the baseline it is measured against is final.
    3. Understand the per-cell collapse — the largest single source of uncertainty in every number above, and nothing currently predicts it.
    4. The deferred optimizer and validation-split experiments, gated behind a multi-seed baseline.
    5. nnU-Net benchmark if the above fall short. Note its default loss is Dice+CE — i.e. this project's baseline — so any margin will come from architecture and schedule.
